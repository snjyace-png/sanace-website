# Narrative Framework — Case Study Storytelling Reference

Distilled from storytelling/communication references Sanjay provided. Only the parts applicable to written portfolio case studies were kept — social-media persuasion tactics and live-speaking frameworks were excluded as not relevant to this format.

Use this as the narrative skeleton for every Tier 1 (storyboarding) and Tier 2 (previz/short film) deep case study.

---

## The 7-Point Story Architecture

1. **Hook** — Disrupt the expected pattern. Open on the most visually or conceptually striking beat of the project, not a chronological intro.
2. **Setup** — Introduce the project/context: what kind of project this was, for whom, what world it lives in.
3. **Inciting Incident** — The specific problem or creative brief that triggered the work.
4. **Struggle** — Show the internal/external conflict: the hard decision, the version that didn't work, the constraint that shaped the outcome. **This is the beat most portfolios skip — prioritize it.** It's also the one Sanjay's WIP/process material naturally supports.
5. **Turning Point** — The insight, guidance, or decision that changed direction.
6. **Transformation** — Action taken, results unfolding. Closest to "final execution," but framed as change happening, not just a gallery of final images.
7. **Resolution + Lesson** — What the project delivered, and what Sanjay took from it. Close with a reflection, not just an outcome statement.

---

## Supporting Principles

**Echo the beginning at the end** — The closing beat (Resolution + Lesson) should return to the Hook's opening image or question. Creates narrative closure instead of the page trailing off.

**Show, don't tell** — Wherever the Struggle and Transformation beats can be conveyed through a sequential image progression (sketch → revision → final; wide → detail) instead of a paragraph, default to the visual sequence. Treat process/WIP images as primary evidence, not supplementary decoration.

**Define what it is / what it is not** (use selectively, not on every project) — A sharp positioning line at the Setup stage can clarify what kind of work this actually is. Example: *"This wasn't concept art — it was a director's shorthand for a scene that didn't exist yet."* Only use where it earns its place.

---

## How the Spine Differs by Tier

- **Tier 1 (Storyboarding):** Struggle/Turning Point center on how Sanjay read the script or scene, the directorial decisions behind panel choices, and collaboration with the director/DP.
- **Tier 2 (Previz / short film):** Struggle/Turning Point center on the production problem previz solved, or — for the short film — the end-to-end ownership story (writing, design, execution).

Each project fills these beats with its own specific content. The 7-point shape is shared; the story inside it is not.
